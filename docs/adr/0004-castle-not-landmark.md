# 4. "Castle" is the canonical name, against four sources saying "Landmark"

Date: 2026-08-25

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The same entity has three names in circulation, and the evidence is lopsided.

Saying "Landmark": the logo wordmark reads "The Queen's Gambit Landmark"; the live
site's title is "Ocean Inspired Art Landmark | TQGL"; the client's contact address is
thequeensgambitlandmark@gmail.com; and the site's own acronym, TQGL, is derived from
it. Saying "House": the legacy domain, thequeensgambithouse.com. Saying "Castle": the
redesign brief, three times — in the Hero attribution, in the Story copy ("Rose The
Queen's Gambit Castle"), and in the Enter section — plus an earlier client mockup
carrying a gold "THE QUEEN'S GAMBIT CASTLE" lockup.

So four independent live sources say Landmark and only the brief says Castle. Read one
way that makes Castle a slip to be corrected. Read another, the brief is the newest
artifact, the client typed Castle deliberately and repeatedly, a Castle logo lockup
already exists, and a redesign pitch is precisely the moment a rebrand gets presented.

The decision was put to the client contact twice — once on the initial conflict, then
again after the live-site title and email address were discovered — and Castle was
confirmed both times.

## Decision

"The Queen's Gambit Castle" is canonical in all visitor-facing copy.

The Monogram is used alone, cropped away from the wordmark, in the header, the Intro
and the Footer. The "Landmark" wordmark is never rendered.

The contact address remains thequeensgambitlandmark@gmail.com, because it is the
client's real working address and inventing one would break the page's only
conversion point.

## Consequences

The Footer will display a mailto address reading "landmark" directly beneath copy
reading "Castle". This is a visible inconsistency that we are choosing to ship
rather than paper over, and it is flagged to the client as a branding item to resolve
— alongside the domain, which reads "house" and matches neither.

If the client resolves in favour of Landmark, the change is three copy strings and the
uncropped wordmark becomes usable. Cheap to reverse.

Adopting Castle discards the existing site's SEO equity in "Landmark" and "TQGL". For
a homepage pitch this costs nothing; before a production launch it is a real question.
